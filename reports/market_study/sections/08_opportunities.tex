\chapter{Opportunities}

This chapter identifies the market gaps and strategic opportunities for
\ProjectName{}, drawing the two parallel research efforts together into a single
ranked view.

\section{Initial Strategic Observation from Market Research Part 1}

The first market research draft suggests that the AMR market is real and
growing, but that the shelf-access retrofit module may be one of the more
expensive and crowded areas of the market.

The same draft suggests that a Tunisia-based team may have early opportunities in
manufacturing intralogistics and sensor or inspection configurations, because
these could use the modular mobile base without requiring major customer facility
changes.

\section{Where \ProjectName{} Fits In}

Mapping the market independently of the team's own product plans, the modular
concept --- a single base platform accepting interchangeable add-ons --- aligns
with the clearest gap above: no competitor reviewed offers one affordable robot
that can be repurposed across delivery, inspection, and patrol simply by swapping
a module.

The opportunity must be stated precisely, however. Modularity already exists at
the top of the market: the MiR base accepts shelf carriers, hooks, pallet lifts,
and an arm. The defensible white space is therefore not modularity in the
abstract, but \textbf{low-cost modularity for SMEs and legacy facilities,
combined with local support} --- a robot that deploys on un-modified premises,
is priced for the local cost-to-labor ratio, and is serviced in French and
Arabic by a local team. The shelf-access module, the most contested and
capital-intensive cell in both matrices, is an opportunity to defer rather than
to lead with.
