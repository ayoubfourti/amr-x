\chapter{Use Cases}

The deployments below are real, documented uses of mobile robots in the field.
They are included as evidence of which configurations have proven value and
which serve as cautionary cases, and each is mapped to the corresponding
\ProjectName{} module configuration.

\section{Industrial Inspection (Sensor Configuration)}

Legged and wheeled inspection robots such as Boston Dynamics' Spot are deployed
in energy and process facilities to carry camera, thermal, and LiDAR payloads on
fixed inspection routes \cite{standardbots_amr}. The deployment requires no
change to the customer's racking or process, and the economic case rests on the
cost of an avoided incident rather than on wages. For \ProjectName{} this
corresponds to a \emph{sensor module on the base} and is the lowest-friction
entry point of any sector.

\section{Healthcare and Internal Delivery (Compartment Configuration)}

MiR and Aethon's TUG have been deployed in hospitals for medication, sample, and
supply transport, addressing the large share of staff time otherwise lost to
internal logistics \cite{mir_healthcare,researchgate_hospital}. The natural
\ProjectName{} fit is a \emph{lockable compartment module on the base} for
secure, auditable transport --- relevant to hospitals, but also to internal
delivery on university and corporate campuses.

\section{Manufacturing Intralogistics (Arm + Base)}

In manufacturing, MiR bases and the Robotnik RB-KAIROS+ (a ROS2 base with a
plug-and-play arm) are used for line-side parts delivery, machine tending, and
kitting \cite{mir250,robotnik_kairos}. This maps to the \ProjectName{}
\emph{arm-on-base configuration} and is the strongest mid-term fit for Tunisia's
export-oriented automotive and electronics suppliers.

\section{Logistics and Fulfillment (Base + Compartment)}

Geek+ goods-to-person fleets are deployed regionally through integrators, for
example at Dubai CommerCity \cite{fdatabot_amr2026}. This is the closest regional
proof point for fulfillment automation and indicates where GCC export demand
sits. For \ProjectName{} the relevant configuration is a \emph{base plus
compartment/sensor} setup, sold via a service model given thin local 3PL
margins.

\section{Security and a Cautionary Case}

Cobalt Robotics operates indoor patrol robots on a subscription model
\cite{coherent_security}, and Tunisia has a notable local precedent in Enova
Robotics' PGuard security UGV (locally built, deployed domestically and
exported; source to be confirmed by the team). Security is best treated as a
\emph{sensor add-on to an already-deployed base} rather than a standalone
product, given a low local guard wage and an entrenched local incumbent.

A cautionary case comes from retail: Bossa Nova's shelf-scanning robots, piloted
at scale by Walmart, were ultimately discontinued by that customer
\cite{dudarenok_retail_china}. It is a reminder that a single-purpose robot with
a weak ROI does not survive contact with a real operating budget --- which is
precisely why a modular base reusable across tasks is the more defensible bet.
